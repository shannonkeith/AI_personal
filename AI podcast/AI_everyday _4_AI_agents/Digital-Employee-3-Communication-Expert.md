# Digital Employee #3: Communication Expert

## Overview
The Communication Expert writes in your voice for your audience, not in generic executive prose. This is where the gap between "this was obviously AI-written" and "this sounds exactly like you" is widest—and entirely within your control.

---

## Core Purpose
- Capture and evolve your unique voice
- Differentiate between audiences and platforms
- Transform rough thinking into polished communication
- Test messaging against specific reader personas
- Provide unlimited iteration without fatigue
- Make your communication reflect your expertise, not bland generics

---

## Setup & Onboarding

### Step 1: Style Profiling

#### Approach A: Analyze Your Existing Writing
Collect your best writing across document types:
- Board updates
- Team emails
- LinkedIn posts
- Public speaking transcripts
- Proposals you're proud of
- Internal strategy docs

Give this collection to your Communication Expert:

> Analyze my writing style across these documents. Tell me:
> 1. What patterns do you notice in how I structure ideas?
> 2. What's my sentence rhythm? (short punchy vs flowing vs mixed)
> 3. What rhetorical devices do I use repeatedly?
> 4. What words/phrases are distinctly mine?
> 5. How do I handle complexity? (simplify, or embrace it?)
> 6. What's my tone? (formal, conversational, witty, direct, warm?)
> 7. How do I begin and end things?

AI is very good at naming patterns you can't articulate yourself. You probably can't describe your writing style linguistically, but AI can.

#### Approach B: Define Your Voice Aspirations
Supplement with writing you admire:

> Here's writing I really like and want to be more like: [examples]
> 
> What do these have in common? How should it influence my voice?

**This isn't copying—it's intentional evolution** of your voice toward where you want it to be.

#### Output: Your Writing Style Guide
Create a document that captures you:

```
MY VOICE PROFILE
Rhythm: Mix of short punchy sentences with occasional longer ones for complex ideas
Tone: Direct but warm, pragmatic but optimistic
Favorite devices: Metaphor, questions, concrete examples
Phrases I use: [list them]
What I avoid: Jargon, false modesty, corporate-speak
How I handle complexity: Simplify first, then add nuance
Sentence length: Average 14-18 words, but ranges 4-35
Structure: Often open with concrete example, then principle
My writing signature: [what makes it distinctly you]
```

### Step 2: Create Reader Personas
You write differently for different people. Define your key audiences:

**For each audience, document:**

```
AUDIENCE: [Name/title]
What they care about:
- What drives their success?
- What keeps them up at night?
- What are they skeptical of?

What they need from you:
- What action do you want them to take?
- What decisions should they make?
- What should they understand?

Their reading style:
- Skim or deep read?
- Want data or intuition?
- Want options or one recommendation?
- Want vision or execution detail?

What would make them stop reading:
- Jargon?
- Too long?
- Wrong level of detail?
```

**Example audiences might be:**
- Your board
- Your executive team
- Your company all-hands
- Your peer group (other founders/executives)
- Your investors
- Your customers

### Step 3: Prime Your Communication Expert
Set up the system:

> You're my Communication Expert. Here's my writing style [share your profile]. Here are my key audiences [share personas].
> 
> When I bring you something to write or refine:
> 1. Know my voice—make it sound like me
> 2. Know my audience—write for them specifically
> 3. Iterate based on my feedback (I'll score on specific dimensions)
> 4. Push back if something doesn't fit my voice
> 5. Challenge me if I'm being too generic

## How to Use: Writing Workflow

### Step 1: Define What You Want (Brief)
Don't just say "write something." Give context:

**Don't say:** "Write a company email about our Q2 results"  
**Do say:** "Write an all-hands email about Q2 results. We missed revenue targets by 12% (caused by sales cycle delays, not product problems). I want the tone to be: honest about the miss but optimistic about what we're learning and how we'll adjust. Audience is a mix of experience levels. Keep it to 300-400 words. End with one clear action I want people to take. Make it sound like me—direct, concrete examples, accessible to everyone."

### Step 2: Give Your Style Guide & Audience Context
Always reference:
- Your writing style guide
- The specific audience persona
- The goal of the communication

> Write this using my voice [guide]. This is for [audience persona]. Here's what I want them to feel and do: [your goal].

### Step 3: Share Your Primer (Initial Thinking)
Never force AI to start from zero:

> Here's what I'm thinking so far...
> 
> [Draft, outline, bullet points, rough ideas—whatever you have]

**Your advantage:** You have context about:
- Company dynamics others don't see
- Subtext that matters
- What you really mean but haven't articulated

Put all of that in.

### Step 4: Get First Draft
The AI creates a version that sounds like you (because it studied your writing).

### Step 5: Feedback Using Dimensions
Don't just say "I don't like it." Score on specific dimensions:

```
DIMENSIONAL FEEDBACK TEMPLATE

Clarity (how easy is it to understand?): 7/10
- Could be clearer on [specific part]
- This paragraph buries the main idea

Tone (does it feel like me?): 8/10
- The opening is perfect
- The middle section feels too formal
- I'd use a stronger verb here than "contribute"

Conciseness (is it tight?): 6/10
- Second paragraph can lose 2 sentences
- The preamble could be shorter
- Last paragraph is just right

Authenticity (would I actually say this?): 7/10
- I'd never use the phrase "[specific phrase]"
- The metaphor about [X] is good
- I'd be more direct about the challenge here
```

**This is much better than "I don't like the tone" because AI can see exactly what to fix.**

### Step 6: Iterate
With dimensional feedback, you get targeted improvements. No endless rewrites.

## Pro Tips for Exceptional Results

### Use Reader Personas to Test
Before finalizing anything, have reader personas review:

> I have three reader personas for this email [persona 1, 2, 3]. Have each answer:
> 1. Is the message clear?
> 2. Would I take action?
> 3. What's missing?
> 4. What would make me stop reading?

Each persona will give you feedback from their specific perspective. Only iterate if multiple personas identify the same issue.

### Evolve Your Voice Intentionally
Your style guide isn't static. Every 6 months, ask:

> Looking at my recent writing, have I evolved? What's changing about how I communicate?

And update your style guide. Your voice should mature along with you.

### Distinguish Across Platforms
You write differently for:
- Email (quick, scannable)
- LinkedIn (professional but personal)
- Slack (casual, quick)
- Board decks (strategic, data-driven)
- Public speaking (conversational, story-driven)
- Writing you're publishing (polished, quotable)

Tell your Communication Expert:

> This is for [platform]. Here's how I write for [this platform]: [example]. Make sure this fits that style.

### Get Specific Feedback Instructions
Tell your Communication Expert exactly what you need:

**For a first draft:** "Give me something to react to; it doesn't need to be perfect"  
**For a final version:** "This goes to the board—make sure it's bulletproof"  
**For quick communication:** "Make it snappy; I don't want people reading more than 1 minute"  
**For emotional impact:** "This is tough news; help me deliver it with empathy but clarity"

### Create a Phrase Library
Keep track of your signature phrases and metaphors:

> You keep using phrases like [X], [Y], [Z] when you talk about [topic]. These are distinctive. Use them in the communication about [new topic] too.

This makes everything sound more consistently you.

## Example Interactions

### Example 1: Board Update
**Your brief:**
> Write a board update on Q2. Here's what happened [provide data and context]. Here's my take: we missed numbers but the underlying business is strong—customer quality actually improved, churn is down, sales cycle extended but conversion stayed solid. 
> 
> Tone: confident but humble. I want them to see the miss as a timing issue, not a fundamental problem. Make it 2 pages max, data-forward but with narrative. Use my voice [reference your style guide]. Audience: [board persona].
> 
> Here's my draft thinking: [your notes]

**AI creates:** A board update that sounds like you, hits the right tone, and positions the narrative the way you intend.

**You review:** Using dimensional feedback ("Confidence is 8/10 but I'd be even more direct about the leading indicators showing strength"), iterate one or two rounds.

**Result:** Polished, authentic communication in 30 minutes instead of 3 hours of writing.

### Example 2: Difficult Email to Team
**Your brief:**
> Write an email to the team about layoffs. This is hard—we need to reduce headcount by 15% due to [business reason]. I want to be respectful and direct. I want people to feel that leadership sees them as people, not just numbers. Tone: honest, empathetic, clear about what happens next.
> 
> Here's what I want them to know: [your thinking]. Here's what I'm NOT willing to do: [boundaries]. Make this sound like me—I'm direct but I care. No corporate BS.
> 
> This will impact [personas]. Make sure each feels the respect here.

**AI creates:** Something you can actually stand behind, not generic corporate language.

### Example 3: Customer Announcement
**Your brief:**
> We're launching a new feature. Write the announcement for [platform]. This is exciting but also means some workflows will change. I want customers to feel like we've thought about their needs, not just built something cool. Medium length—could be read in 2 minutes. Make it conversational, not marketing-y.
> 
> Here's the feature [description]. Here's why it matters [your framing]. Use my voice [guide]. Audience: [customer persona].

## Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Why It Matters | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Generic style guide | AI produces generic writing | Spend time on actual style profiling |
| Vague feedback | Multiple iterations, no progress | Use dimensional scoring always |
| No audience context | Writing misses | Share persona with every brief |
| No primer from you | Misses your actual thinking | Always share your initial thoughts |
| Never testing with personas | You assume it works until it doesn't | Always run through reader personas before sending |
| Treating all writing same | Different formats need different approaches | Specify platform and context |
| Ignoring your signature voice | Becomes generic despite effort | Reference your phrase library |
| Endless iteration | Never good enough | Use dimensional feedback to avoid this |

## Maturity Levels

**Level 1: Assisted Writing**
- AI helps you write something
- Rough style guide
- One or two reader audiences

**Level 2: Voice-Matched Writing**
- Detailed style profile
- Multiple audience personas
- Dimensional feedback on iterations
- Consistent voice across communications

**Level 3: Strategic Communication**
- Style guide updated regularly
- 5+ well-defined audience personas
- Integrated with other digital employees
- Testing against personas as standard
- Archive of communication for reference

**Level 4: Communication as Differentiation**
- Your communication is known for being authentic, clear, distinct
- Used internally as template for organizational communication
- Phrase library is part of company culture
- You've become more articulate and persuasive through intentional practice
- Audiences seek out your communications specifically

---

## Success Metrics

You'll know your Communication Expert is working when:

- **Speed:** You write faster because you don't draft endlessly
- **Authenticity:** People say "this sounds like you"
- **Clarity:** Your message lands the way you intended
- **Impact:** Your communication actually drives action
- **Confidence:** You're not second-guessing tone or phrasing
- **Consistency:** Your voice is recognizable across platforms
- **Evolution:** Your writing gets better over time
- **Efficiency:** Fewer rounds of feedback to get to polished version

---

## Quick Start (2 Hours)

1. **45 min:** Collect your writing, have AI profile your style
2. **30 min:** Define 3-5 key audience personas
3. **20 min:** Create your writing style guide
4. **25 min:** Write something real with your Communication Expert

You'll immediately feel the difference between "this was written by AI" and "this is my voice, amplified."
