# AI Daily Brief - Executive AI Catch-Up Program Overview

**Episode:** Executive AI Catch-Up Program with Nifar Gaspar  
**Topic:** Framework for executives to effectively adopt and implement AI tools

---

## The Problem: Capability Overhang

A significant gap exists between frontier companies discussing agent orchestration and the broader executive population feeling left behind. This creates three common executive archetypes:

- **Podcast CTO**: Highly knowledgeable about AI releases and benchmarks but hasn't built any systems themselves
- **Weekend Tinkerer**: Builds impressive projects in private time but doesn't know how to apply them to day-to-day work
- **Manifesto Writer**: Has clear strategic vision and has funded transformation efforts but doesn't personally execute the work

**Critical insight:** A leader's quality of AI usage is the single biggest predictor of how well their teams adopt AI.

---

## 5 Non-Negotiable Operating Principles

These apply across all AI tools and workflows:

### 1. Use Voice Mode Over Typing
- Typing filters thinking; speaking reveals intuitive insights
- Modern AI models handle unstructured, non-linear input extremely well
- Your intuitive thinking is more valuable than polished input

### 2. Brain Dump Regularly
- Executives carry enormous undocumented context: relationship dynamics, meeting undercurrents, half-formed intuitions
- Capture this context habitually (voice notes, meeting reflections, quick brain dumps)
- Reduce friction to make capturing thoughts a regular habit

### 3. Have AI Interview You
- The more senior you are, the more unknown unknowns you have
- Ask AI to surface assumptions and blind spots before executing major tasks
- Example questions: "What am I assuming? What haven't I considered? What context should I provide?"

### 4. Separate Planning from Execution
- For critical tasks, don't jump straight to output
- Have a conversation with AI to plan: What information is needed? What's the order? What does success look like?
- Only execute after planning is refined (can be in separate conversation/tool)

### 5. Be Intentional About Intervention Points
- Don't fully offload judgment-heavy work to AI
- Identify where your judgment adds the most value
- Design systems so AI handles everything else; you step in at strategic moments
- Always capture initial thoughts/primers—this is where your experience advantage lies

## 4 Digital Employees to Hire

### 1. Research Analyst
**The one you always wanted but never had time to properly manage**

**Key techniques:**
- **Be opinionated upfront**: State your decision in mind, existing assumptions, and constraints
- **Specify scope**: Define time horizons, sources that matter, what to exclude, what constitutes reliable data
- **Wisdom of the Crowd**: Run the same research across multiple AI models/sessions, aggregate where they agree, investigate divergence
- **Fact-check separately**: AI is better at verifying than generating correct research from scratch
- **Three validation questions before acting:**
  - Is this grounded in real sources or just AI pattern matching?
  - What's missing that I didn't think to ask?
  - Am I comfortable putting my name to it?

**Pro tips:**
- 100% consensus across tools = likely true fact
- Only one tool reports something = requires deeper research
- Format output creatively (interactive dashboards, infographics, audio summaries) not just text

---

### 2. Strategic Thought Partner
**The advisor available 24/7 with no ego and infinite patience**

**Key techniques:**
- **Build a personal context system**: Document your role, company, ecosystem, competitive stance, priorities, decision history
- **Create a board of advisors**: Not just one AI voice—build personas of mentors/thought leaders you respect
- **Have them debate decisions**: Get diverse perspectives before they present to you
- **Calibrate pushback**: Challenge you when needed, but don't be purely sycophantic or devil's advocate for sport
- **Match your decision style**: Some leaders need 10 options, others want one bottom line after debate, some want hard pushback first

**Pro tips:**
- Before major decisions, ask AI to surface biases (yours and its own)
- Run scenario simulations: "What if the market shifts?" "What if a competitor does X?"
- Stress-test decisions under multiple futures, not just the one you hope happens

---

### 3. Communication Expert
**The writer who captures your voice for your audience**

**Key techniques:**
- **Style profiling**: Collect your best writing across documents (board updates, team emails, LinkedIn posts), have AI analyze your patterns
- **Create reader personas**: Define what different audiences care about, what drives them to action, what they're skeptical of
- **Have personas review drafts**: Ask them: Is the message clear? Would I take action? What's missing? What would make me stop reading?
- **Give specific dimensional feedback**: Don't say "I don't like this"—score on clear dimensions (e.g., 9/10 clarity, 5/10 wittiness, 7/10 conciseness)

**Pro tips:**
- Make qualitative feedback very concrete (what about the sentence structure? phrasing? ideas?)
- Test against reader personas repeatedly to avoid endless iterations
- Supplement with writing you admire to evolve your style beyond exact replication

---

### 4. Operational Powerhouse
**The tireless operator who monitors everything without fatigue**

**Key techniques:**
- **Think bigger than current automation**: Don't just automate what you already do
- **Ask**: What visibility/operations did I always want but never had bandwidth for?
  - Daily departmental overviews?
  - Deep P&L analysis every morning?
  - Stakeholder relationship tracker with context pre-meeting?
  - Multi-channel status synthesis?
- **Personalize, don't generalize**: Generic meeting prep isn't enough—include your specific undercurrents, relevant sources, stakeholder CRM insights
- **Test manually before automating**: Run the workflow manually for 1-2 weeks before committing to automation
- **Capture intangibles**: Document undercurrents, relationship history, anything not in official CRM systems

**Pro tips:**
- Define dashboards/automations as: "What would I build with unlimited headcount?"
- Test in stealth mode first (doesn't impact decisions) before full automation
- Use stakeholder relationship tracking to prepare for every meeting

## Core Principles Across All Four Employees

| Principle | Impact |
|-----------|--------|
| **Context is everything** | The more AI knows about you, the better it performs. Generic output → specific, personalized output |
| **Tool choice < Methodology** | Focus on what you're building and how, not which specific tool |
| **Opinionated input** | Your judgment, experience, and assumptions steer AI toward useful output |
| **Separate thinking from execution** | Planning first prevents poor decisions down the line |
| **Personal CRMAKA** | Your personal repository of context/history is invaluable |

---

## Critical Success Factors

1. **Time investment**: You can't do meaningful AI work with 15 minutes/week—carve out dedicated time
2. **Personal usage first**: Model the behavior you want your team to adopt
3. **Build one system at a time**: Get mileage with each digital employee before adding the next
4. **Test before automating**: Validate manual workflows before committing to automation
5. **Evolve gradually**: Start with these four; eventually build a "chief of staff" AI that orchestrates across all of them

---

## Next Steps

1. **Pick one** of the four digital employees to implement
2. **Apply the principles** we've covered
3. **Refine through actual usage** with real decisions and workflows
4. **Expand** to other employees once you've built proficiency

The goal is not to learn about AI from the shore, but to get in the water with intention and start swimming.

## Key Insight

**Your quality of AI usage directly predicts how well your team adopts AI.**
- When the CEO is the best user → organization becomes the most forward-thinking
- When leaders hand off decisions → team either underestimates or sets unrealistic expectations

Roll up your sleeves. Don't rely on others to do everything for you. Become a better AI user, which makes you a better leader of AI adoption.
