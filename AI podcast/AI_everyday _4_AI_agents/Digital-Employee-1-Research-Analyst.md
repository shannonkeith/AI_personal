# Digital Employee #1: Research Analyst

## Overview
The Research Analyst is your on-demand research expert who conducts deep analysis with your specific decision-making context in mind. Unlike generic searches, this employee understands your constraints, prioritizes, and validates information.

---

## Core Purpose
- Conduct opinionated research aligned with your decision needs
- Eliminate generic results through specific briefing
- Validate findings through multiple perspectives
- Surface hidden gaps and assumptions

---

## Setup & Onboarding

### Step 1: Define Your Research Style
Before your first research request, establish baseline preferences:

**Create a research briefing template:**
```
DECISION CONTEXT:
- What decision am I trying to make?
- What am I already assuming?
- What's at stake?

CONSTRAINTS:
- Time horizon relevant to my decision (e.g., 3-12 months)
- Budget/resource constraints
- Competitive/regulatory environment

SOURCE PRIORITIES:
- Primary sources (academic papers, data, original research)
- Secondary sources (industry reports, analyses)
- Sources to exclude (marketing materials, outdated benchmarks)
- Reliability criteria in my domain

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- What would make this research valuable?
- How will I use these findings?
```

### Step 2: Prime Your Analyst
In your first interaction, provide context:

> I'm your research analyst. I need your help researching [TOPIC]. Here's how I like to work:
> - I come to research with these assumptions: [list them]
> - I prioritize sources in this order: [your priority order]
> - Never use [these types of sources] as primary sources
> - I need findings presented as [format: brief, detailed, visual, etc.]
> - This research will inform [your specific decision]

## How to Use: 3-Step Workflow

### Step 1: Brief Your Analyst (Opinionated Input)
State your decision and constraints clearly:

**Don't say:** "Research AI trends"  
**Do say:** "I'm evaluating whether to invest in agentic systems for our customer support team over the next 18 months. I'm skeptical about the maturity level. Please research: (1) Current production use cases outside of tech companies, (2) Cost-benefit data from companies with similar support volume, (3) Implementation timelines and risks. Exclude vendor marketing materials. Focus on recent case studies (last 12 months) and peer analysis."

**Your advantage:** You know your business constraints better than any AI tool. Use that.

### Step 2: Apply Wisdom of the Crowd
Don't trust a single model's research:

1. **Run the same research across multiple AI models** (Claude, GPT-4, Gemini, etc.) or same model in separate sessions
2. **Aggregate results**: Where do they agree? Where do they diverge?
3. **Investigate divergence**: When only one model reports something, that's a flag
4. **Fact-check the aggregation**: Use a separate model to verify aggregated findings

**Confidence scoring:**
- 100% consensus across models = likely factual
- 3+ models agree = reliable
- 1-2 models only = requires deeper investigation
- Divergent results = acknowledge uncertainty in your decision

### Step 3: Validation Before Acting
Before you use the research, ask yourself these three questions:

1. **Grounded or pattern-matching?** Is this based on real sources or just AI pattern matching?
2. **What's missing?** What didn't I think to ask?
3. **Would I sign my name to it?** Am I genuinely comfortable standing behind this?

**If you intuitively say "no" to #3**, even without good justification, pause and do more work. This catches ~80% of AI research failures before they become bad decisions.

## Pro Tips for Exceptional Results

### Format for Consumption, Not Just Reading
- **Interactive dashboards** over static reports
- **Infographics** over tables
- **Audio summaries** for commute listening
- **Filtered/sortable databases** for ongoing reference
- **Visual comparisons** when evaluating options

Ask AI: *"How should I interact with this data? What format would be most useful?"*

### Create a Research Archive
Build a living document of research findings organized by:
- Topic/decision area
- Date conducted
- Key findings
- Confidence level
- Source diversity score
- How findings were used

This becomes your organizational memory.

### Establish Domain Reliability Criteria
What counts as "reliable data" varies by domain:

**For market research:**
- Independent analyst firms (not vendor-sponsored)
- Academic peer-reviewed studies
- Public company earnings calls
- Customer surveys with methodology disclosed

**For technical decisions:**
- GitHub activity / real usage metrics
- Benchmarks with methodology
- Production case studies
- RFC discussions / design docs

**For business decisions:**
- Primary source data (your metrics)
- Peer companies (if accessible)
- Industry association reports
- Analyst coverage

Tell your Research Analyst upfront what counts in your world.

### Handling Conflicting Information
When models disagree, follow this process:

1. Document the disagreement clearly
2. Ask: "Why might Model A say X and Model B say Y?"
3. Identify which used more recent data
4. Check if they're answering slightly different questions
5. Make a provisional decision with uncertainty noted
6. Plan to revisit as new data emerges

## Example Interactions

### Example 1: Market Research
**Your brief:**
> I'm considering entering the European B2B SaaS market for contract management. Research the current competitive landscape. I need: (1) Top 5 competitors by market share, (2) Their pricing models, (3) Key differentiators customers care about, (4) Regulatory differences vs US that affect product strategy. Use analyst reports and customer reviews as primary sources. No marketing websites. I'll use this for a board update, so keep it executive-level but substantive.

**After getting results, you:**
1. Run same research with 2-3 other models
2. Compare findings on competitive positioning
3. Ask yourself: "Would I present this to the board with confidence?"
4. If yes, format as board-ready presentation
5. If no, identify gaps and do targeted follow-up research

### Example 2: Internal Data Analysis
> I want to understand why our customer retention dropped 8% in Q2. Research our historical patterns: (1) Correlation with any product changes/pricing changes, (2) Cohort analysis - which customer segments were affected most, (3) External factors that might have influenced (market conditions, competitor moves), (4) Leading indicators we should have caught it earlier. Pull from our CRM, analytics, and customer feedback. Help me understand the root cause, not just the symptom.

## Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Why It Matters | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Generic research query | AI returns generic results | Always include your constraints and decision context |
| Single model validation | One AI's bias becomes your bias | Use wisdom of the crowd (3+ models) |
| Skipping the "sign my name" test | You'll use mediocre research | Take 30 seconds for validation before acting |
| Using marketing as primary source | Vendor claims aren't objective data | Explicitly exclude during briefing |
| Never updating findings | Market changes, research becomes stale | Re-run research quarterly for ongoing decisions |
| Accepting wall-of-text output | You won't actually use it | Ask for interactive/visual formats |

## Integration with Other Digital Employees

**With Strategic Thought Partner:** Feed research findings to advisors; have them debate implications  
**With Operational Powerhouse:** Automate recurring research (competitive analysis, market tracking)  
**With Communication Expert:** Use research analyst output as source material for executive communications

---

## Maturity Levels

**Level 1: Basic Research**
- Ask single research question
- Accept first response
- No validation

**Level 2: Opinionated Research**
- Brief with constraints and context
- Use wisdom of the crowd (2-3 models)
- Apply the three validation questions

**Level 3: Systematic Research**
- Maintain research archive by topic
- Establish reliability criteria for your domain
- Integrate with other digital employees
- Automate recurring research (e.g., weekly competitive monitoring)

**Level 4: Research as Strategic Advantage**
- Research becomes input to scenario planning
- Build dashboards that synthesize ongoing research
- Create decision frameworks informed by research patterns
- Become known for data-driven insights internally

---

## Success Metrics

You'll know your Research Analyst is working when:
- You make decisions faster (research didn't take 3 weeks)
- You feel confident in your research (passed the "sign my name" test)
- Your findings are actionable (not just interesting)
- Your team trusts your analysis (because it's substantiated)
- Research surfaces unexpected patterns (the AI found something you wouldn't have asked about)
